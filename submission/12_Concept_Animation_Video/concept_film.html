Verification of the film's solar model

The film computes sun angles in the browser; the project's published figures come from pvlib running the NREL algorithm in Python. The two were checked against each other before this was published:

64.75°
Equinox noon altitude — Python gives 64.8°
0.19 m
6 m tree shadow at 88.2° — identical
16.48 m
Same tree at 20° — identical
4,364
Annual daylight hours — Python gives 4,402

The 0.9% gap in daylight hours is expected and explainable: pvlib reports apparent altitude, which includes atmospheric refraction and keeps the sun visible a few minutes longer at each end of the day. The film uses true geometric altitude. Neither is wrong — they answer slightly different questions, and the difference never exceeds one degree.

Where the canopy still loses

The same model, evaluated on the 7 m walk beneath its 18 m gridshell at 4.5 m, with the 3 m southern louvre:

100%
21 June, noon — sun at 87.3°, walk fully covered
84%
21 December, 09:00 — sun at 26.8°. The louvre is what holds this; the plane alone loses the path entirely
74%
21 December, 15:00 — sun at 22.6° in the south-west. This is the worst case, and it is not fixed by more structure

December is the weakest month at 72% mean coverage, and 56 hours a year leave the walk with no shade anywhere along it. The straight canopy this scheme replaced left 330. That reduction is what the arc buys, and it costs about a point of mean coverage — which is the trade, stated in the direction that is not flattering.
