# Phase 1.07 — Environmental Analysis
Al Safa 2 Park

## Ecology & Biodiversity
- Existing tree canopy (visible west portion of site, per master-plan graphic) is the primary
  existing ecological asset — mature trees in Dubai's residential parks are typically Ghaf
  (Prosopis cineraria - UAE national tree), Neem, Ficus, and date palm plantings; species
  cannot be confirmed without the DWG planting layer or a site visit.
- No water bodies visible on site — no aquatic habitat present currently.
- Being an isolated urban green patch surrounded by roads/residential fabric, the park likely
  functions as a **stepping-stone habitat** for birds and urban-tolerant species rather than a
  primary habitat corridor.

## Water
- No existing water features identified in available materials.
- Dubai's water context (from Climate Analysis, Phase 1.05): near-zero rainfall Jun–Sep,
  light rainfall Dec–Feb (~15-23mm/month) — any landscape strategy must assume irrigation-
  dependent planting, reinforcing the brief's explicit call for "water-sensitive landscape
  design" and "sustainable irrigation strategies."

## Vegetation
- Visible canopy concentration = west side of site; east side reads more open/hardscape in the
  aerial graphic. This asymmetry is a genuine existing condition worth carrying into Phase 2
  (Problem Definition) as a potential shade-equity issue.

## Soil
- No soil/geotechnical data provided. Dubai's residential belt (Al Safa/Umm Suqeim) is
  typically built on calcareous sandy soils common to the emirate — a reasonable regional
  assumption, but not site-specific data. Logged as a gap.

## Stormwater
- No stormwater infrastructure data available (would sit in the DWG utilities layer, pending
  conversion per Phase 1.03).
- Given Dubai's rare but intense rainfall events (e.g., short intense winter storms), a
  stormwater/drainage strategy is a known citywide design concern worth flagging for Phase 7
  (Performance & Sustainability) — not resolved here.

## Carbon
- No baseline carbon data exists for the current park. A carbon baseline (existing tree
  sequestration vs. proposed) would need to be modeled once species/quantities are known
  (post-DWG conversion or post-planting-strategy phase).

## Habitat
- Existing canopy patch likely supports common urban bird species (mynas, doves, sparrows)
  typical of Dubai's older residential parks; no formal survey data available.

## Noise
- Sheikh Zayed Road (E11) borders the site's east edge — a major arterial highway is a
  significant, confirmed noise source requiring acoustic buffering (planting berms, distance,
  or built form) in later design phases.

## Air
- No air quality monitoring data available for the specific site. Proximity to SZR traffic is
  a reasonable qualitative concern for air quality at the park's eastern edge.

## Visual Corridors
- From the master-plan graphic: the Dubai skyline (visible in the Neighborhood Parks Manual's
  generic cover imagery, not this specific site) is not confirmed as visible from Al Safa 2
  Park itself — no skyline sightline data specific to this site is available.
- Internally, the visible circular plaza feature near the park's center appears to be a
