PHASE 1.06 - SHADOW ANALYSIS - AL SAFA 2 PARK, DUBAI
=======================================================

METHOD:
  Shadow length = object height / tan(solar elevation angle)
  Solar elevation/azimuth computed exactly for site lat/lon via pvlib
  (NREL Solar Position Algorithm), for 2026 solstices/equinox.

KEY FINDINGS:
  - Summer noon: sun near-zenith (elev ~88 deg) -> shadows extremely short
    (~0.2m for a 6m tree) - vertical shade structures/canopy needed, not
    reliance on low objects, for midday summer shade.
  - Winter noon: sun lower (elev ~41 deg) -> shadows much longer
    (~6.9m for a 6m tree) - south-facing facades/walls cast usable shade.
  - Morning/evening in all seasons: long shadows from low sun angles,
    directionally from the east (morning) / west (evening).
  - Shadow direction at noon swings from just-north-of-overhead (summer)
    to due-north (winter) in this northern-hemisphere low-latitude site.

DESIGN IMPLICATIONS (for later phases, not decided yet):
  - Overhead/canopy shade (pergolas, tree canopy, shade sails) is essential
    for summer midday comfort since low structures cast almost no shadow then.
  - East-west oriented paths will be shaded by roadside/building shadow in
    morning and evening; north-south paths get least natural shade at those times.
  - Seating/gathering nodes on the north side of tall elements (trees, structures)
    benefit most consistently across all three seasons.
