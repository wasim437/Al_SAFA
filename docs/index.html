A correction this submission makes to itself

An earlier version claimed 99.2% annual shade on the spine. It does not survive a geometric check, and it has been withdrawn.

Why the old number was wrong

A flat 9 m canopy at 5.5 m does not shade a 9 m walkway when the sun is low and to the south — the shadow slides off the path. Modelled properly, hour by hour, across all 4,402 annual daylight hours:

ModelAnnual shade on the spine
Previously claimed99.2%
Canopy structure alone60.18%
Canopy + flanking tree avenue69.6%

The honest number is lower — and it is the better argument. It is what shows the tree avenue is structural to the concept rather than decoration. A scheme claiming 99% invites one juror with a sun-path diagram to discredit everything else. This one invites them to check the working.

The machine learning

Four models. The constraint on all of them: the target must not be recoverable from the inputs by algebra. The heat index is a closed-form function of temperature and humidity, so "predicting" it from temperature and humidity is arithmetic wearing a lab coat.

Honesty notes

The full version is in DATA_SOURCES.md.

  • The climate record is 39 years (NCM 1977–2015) but published as monthly normals. The 8,760-hour series is reconstructed from it, verified back against those normals to within 0.39 °C, and labelled MODELLED everywhere it appears. Conclusions are about a typical year, never about extremes.
  • The site boundary is assumed — a 150 × 100 m rectangle pending confirmation against the supplied DWG. Every area figure depends on it.
  • Visitor demand is a scenario model, not a machine learning result. It is deliberately excluded from the model suite: a model trained on it would only recover the assumption that produced it.
  • Diffuse radiation is excluded from the shade model, so shaded comfort is stated conservatively.
