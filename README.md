## The scheme, in one line

One arc. A crescent of shade 141 m in radius sweeps across the site; a water
channel runs beneath its northern drip line; and every room in the park is
struck off the same centre, so no room is a rectangle and every room faces the
crescent square-on.

## Why the plan is an arc

This is the part of the design that was solved rather than styled.

A straight canopy presents **one orientation**. When a sun angle defeats it, it
defeats the entire length at once, and the walk has no shade anywhere along it.
An arc changes heading continuously, so some segment is always angled well.

The depth of the bow was swept against the 8,760-hour solar model at a fixed
section, over all 4,402 daylight hours of the year:

| Plan form | Mean cover | Worst month | Hours with **no shade anywhere** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Straight east–west bar | **87.4%** | 68.7% | 330 |
| Sine meander *(superseded)* | 85.0% | 73.1% | 63 |
| Arc, sagitta 14 m | 86.6% | 71.3% | 62 |
| **Arc, sagitta 18 m** | 85.9% | **72.1%** | **52** ← adopted |
| Arc, sagitta 22 m | 84.9% | 72.3% | 61 |
| Closed elliptical loop | 79.1% | 69.8% | 89 |

**Read the first column carefully.** The straight bar has the *highest* mean
coverage of anything tested — an east–west canopy is close to the optimum
orientation for 25°N, and curving it costs about a point. The crescent is not
claimed to shade more ground on average. It is claimed to remove the hours in
which the route offers nowhere at all to stand, and it removes six sevenths of
them. That is the trade, stated in the direction that is not flattering.

The closed loop loses on every measure, for the same reason the deep bows do: it
forces half its length to run north–south, and a canopy over a north–south route
can only work when the sun is low in the east or west. The loop survives in the
scheme as **Al Madar**, an *unshaded* running circuit — because that is what a
circuit is actually for.

The arc bows **convex south**. The solar model is indifferent to that sign, so
the choice was spatial: bowing south puts the structure between the sun and the
hollow it wraps, making the concave side the park's cool pocket instead of a
south-facing bowl. Every room people are asked to *linger* in sits in that
hollow, and [`figures/fig04_site_comfort_map.png`](figures/fig04_site_comfort_map.png)
is the check on that claim.

## ⚠️ Two corrections this submission makes to itself

**1. A withdrawn shade claim.** An earlier version claimed **99.2%** annual shade
on a flat 9 m canopy over a 9 m walkway. It does not survive a geometric check —
when the sun is low and to the south, the shadow slides clean off the path — and
it was withdrawn. The section was then re-solved: a narrower walk, a wider
overhang, a lower plane, and a deep southern louvre. That section now measures
**87.3%**, and it is a number that can be checked.

**2. Three withdrawn images.** Three visuals presented invented data as
measurement: a "PET / CFD thermal comfort analysis" sourced to *CFD Simulation
Data* when no CFD was ever run, a "Satellite Remote Sensing NDVI Analytics" pair
whose raster is numpy noise, and a "Generative Parametric Voronoi Canopy Mesh —
Algorithmic Solar Optimization" in which nothing was optimised. All three are
withdrawn on the same principle as the 99.2%: anything that cannot survive being
checked should not be in front of a juror. See
[`archive/withdrawn_visuals/README.md`](archive/withdrawn_visuals/README.md).

## Why there is code in a landscape architecture submission

The brief asks how AI supported the design. This repository is that answer, and
it is meant to be *run*, not just read.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m tests.test_pipeline    # 33 correctness checks
python run_analysis.py           # datasets, models, figures — end to end (~3 min)
python -m src.drawings           # section, elevation, circulation, planting
python -m src.boards             # the two presentation boards
node docs/_PORTAL/selftest.js    # 62 portal checks
node tests/test_film.js          # every frame of the concept film
```

Python here does exactly one job: **computation on data**. It loads sources,
models physical processes, trains models and draws figures. It does not write
prose and it does not typeset documents — the written reports are authored in
Word, by a person, which is where writing belongs.

### The plan is generated, not drawn

[`src/plan.py`](src/plan.py) is the single source of the masterplan geometry.
Every room is a box in the crescent's own polar frame, mapped to site metres and
clipped to the boundary. Two things follow:

- **Areas are measured, not authored.** Each zone's area is the shoelace area of
  the polygon actually drawn, which is why the schedule closes on 15,000 m²
  without anyone reconciling a spreadsheet.
- **The figures, the portal and the film read the same geometry.** Change the
  sagitta and the masterplan, the section, the circulation diagram, the planting
  plan, the 3D model and the sixty-second film all move with it — or the tests
  fail loudly.

## The machine learning

Four models. The design constraint on all of them: **the target must not be
recoverable from the inputs by algebra.** It is easy to score R² = 1.000 on this
project by accident — the heat index is a closed-form function of temperature and
humidity, so "predicting" it from temperature and humidity is arithmetic wearing
a lab coat.

| Model | Task | Result | Why it is a real problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1a** Random Forest | Shade surrogate (regression) | **R² 0.998** | Learns a slow ray-traced simulation from cheap plan geometry |
| **M1b** Neural network | Shade surrogate (deployed) | **R² 0.994** | Differentiable → usable inside a layout optimiser; a forest is not |
| **M2** Gradient Boosting | Comfort band (4-class) | **97.5%** acc | Temperature and humidity **withheld** — it sees only sun position and the calendar |
| **M3** K-Means | Microclimate regimes | k=2 by silhouette | Unsupervised; k is *selected*, not chosen to look tidy |

**M1 is the flagship.** Ray-tracing annual shade means projecting 131 tree
shadows onto 15,000 ground cells for every daylight hour — slow, and it must be
re-run for *every* design variation. That cost is what stops a designer
exploring. The surrogate answers in milliseconds instead of minutes.

**M2 answers a question worth asking.** If thermal stress is predictable from a
clock and an ephemeris alone, park operations can be scheduled without a sensor
network — a real capital and maintenance saving. At 97.5% accuracy, it is.

## What the models actually changed about the design

Analysis that does not change a drawing is decoration.

| Model output | Design consequence |
|---|---|
| A straight route has no shade anywhere for 330 hours a year; an 18 m arc has 52 | The plan is **an arc**, and the sagitta is the value that minimises that column — not the one that maximises mean coverage |
| A closed loop scores worst on every measure | The circuit is kept, but **unshaded** — Al Madar is a running loop, not a second canopy |
| The concave side of the crescent is measurably cooler than the convex side | The basin, the quiet garden, the children's play and the picnic grove are **all placed in the hollow**; the sports lawn, the plaza and the souk take the convex face |
| Permutation importance puts **distance to the crescent** far above every other feature | Confirms the shade budget belongs in one continuous element rather than dispersed — but see the next row, because this was not always the answer |
| Under the *superseded* layout the top feature was **tree density within 20 m** | The finding moved when the design moved. `fig06`'s caption is generated from the model output for exactly this reason: a hard-coded conclusion would now be a lie |
| Comfort is ~97% predictable from sun and calendar | **No sensor network.** Programming runs off an almanac |
| Comfort gain concentrates in **late afternoon, spring and autumn** | Activation targets those windows; summer midday is not programmed outdoors |
| Site-wide mean shade is modest (34.1%) | Stated as a deliberate position: this scheme makes **a few places genuinely excellent**, rather than the whole site marginally better |
| An open water channel in Dubai evaporates | The falaj is set on the canopy's **drip line**, shaded all day, rather than in the open |

## Honesty notes

The full version is [`DATA_SOURCES.md`](DATA_SOURCES.md). The short version:

- The **climate record is 39 years** (NCM 1977–2015) but is published as **monthly
  normals**. The 8,760-hour series is **reconstructed** from it, verified back
  against those normals to within **0.39 °C**, and labelled `MODELLED` everywhere
  it appears. Conclusions are about a *typical* year, never about extremes.
- The **site boundary is assumed** — a 150 × 100 m rectangle pending confirmation
  against the supplied DWG. Every area figure depends on it.
- **Visitor demand is a scenario model, not a machine learning result.** It is
  deliberately excluded from the model suite: a model trained on it would only
  recover the assumption that produced it, and reporting that as a finding would
  be dishonest.
- **Diffuse radiation is excluded** from the shade model, so shaded comfort is
  stated conservatively.
- The **6 °C shade relief** is a literature value, not a site measurement.
- The **renders are AI-generated** and illustrate the design language. They are
  not photographs of a built thing, and none of them is presented as analysis.
